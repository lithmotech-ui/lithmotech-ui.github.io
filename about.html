About Lithmo

Building India-ready laboratory robotics for accessible automation.

Lithmo Technologies Private Limited is an Indian robotics and laboratory automation startup focused on developing programmable liquid handling systems for diagnostic, biotech, academic, and research laboratories.

Our mission

To make laboratory automation more accessible to Indian laboratories by building affordable, programmable, and practical robotic systems for routine liquid handling workflows.

Our vision

To help build stronger domestic scientific infrastructure by reducing dependence on expensive imported automation systems and enabling more laboratories to adopt practical robotics.

Founder

Lithmo is founder-led and built from direct experience with laboratory workflows, biotechnology research, and automation-driven product development.

Why Lithmo was started

Lithmo was started to address a practical gap in Indian laboratories: many labs need automation, but available systems are often expensive, imported, and not always accessible to smaller institutions, early-stage labs, and price-sensitive research environments.

Current development stage

Lithmo is beyond the ideation stage and is currently at the proof-of-concept / prototype-development stage.

Validated foundation

Control electronics and motor actuation have been validated, establishing the motion-control foundation needed for the liquid-handling platform.

Prototype under construction

The integrated liquid-handling prototype is under construction, including mechanical assembly, pipette positioning, and software-controlled workflow development.

Working toward MVP

The next milestone is a functional integrated MVP suitable for demonstration, refinement, pilot workflow discussions, and grant-supported product development.

What Lithmo stands for

Lithmo is being built around practical engineering, scientific credibility, affordability, and India-focused laboratory needs.

Scientific utility

Products should solve real laboratory workflow problems, not merely demonstrate robotics for its own sake.

Accessible automation

Automation should be available to more laboratories, not only institutions with large capital budgets.

Local relevance

The product strategy is focused on Indian laboratory conditions, budgets, serviceability, and user needs.
